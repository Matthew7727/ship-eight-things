What this is

How the guide works

This isn't a course. There are no exercises with tidy answers, no progress bars, no certificates. You learn by building things that don't work yet — and making them work.

Each of the eight projects is designed so the next one is impossible without learning something new. The frustration at each step is the curriculum. The guide explains why things work, not just what to type.

What you'll have at the end

  • Eight repositories with four months of daily commit history
  • At least three applications live on the internet with real URLs
  • A working understanding of Python, the terminal, git, HTTP, SQL, deployment, and building with language models
  • The habit of getting yourself unstuck — which is the thing that actually makes someone employable
The one rule

You must be able to explain every line you commit. Not "it works." Why it works. If you can't explain it, it doesn't get committed yet. This rule sounds annoying. It's the whole point.

How the detail level changes

In the early sessions of Project 1, every line of code is explained. Every terminal command comes with a plain-English translation. Nothing is assumed.

By Project 4 you'll be going back to fix your own code from two weeks earlier — and by that point you won't need everything spelled out. The guide scales back the hand-holding gradually, in line with what you should know by then.

If something goes unexplained and you don't understand it, that's the cue to look it up — that's also part of the job.
